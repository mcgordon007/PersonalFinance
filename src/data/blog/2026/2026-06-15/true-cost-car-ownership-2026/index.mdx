## What AAA Says You Are Actually Paying

The American Automobile Association estimates that the average American driver spends between $10,000 and $12,000 per year on vehicle ownership for a typical sedan driven 15,000 miles annually. For trucks and SUVs, the number runs higher. For new cars in urban areas with high insurance costs, it can exceed $15,000. Break that down: $12,000 per year is $1,000 per month, or roughly $0.60 per mile. Most people do not think about cost per mile they think about the payment. AAA cost categories include: depreciation (the largest single cost, averaging 30-35% of total ownership cost over five years), fuel, insurance, maintenance and repair, licensing and registration, and financing costs.

## Depreciation: The Invisible Bill You Cannot Avoid

New cars lose value the moment they leave the lot. A new car typically loses 20-25% of its value in the first year, and roughly 60% by the fifth year. This is not an opinion it is how the market works. A $40,000 car that is worth $32,000 the day you drive it home will be worth roughly $24,000 by year three and $16,000 by year five. Depreciation is the single largest cost of car ownership. When you finance a car, you are paying interest on a depreciating asset. You owe more than the car is worth for the first two to three years of a typical 6-year loan being upside down on the loan. The practical implication: buying a three-year-old certified pre-owned car instead of a brand-new car can save you $10,000 to $15,000 in depreciation. The previous owner absorbed the steepest part of the depreciation curve.

## Insurance, Fuel, and Maintenance

Car insurance averages $1,100 to $1,400 per year nationally for full coverage as of 2025-2026. Your premium depends on age, location, driving record, and the car you drive. The gap between the cheapest and most expensive insurers for the same driver can be $1,000 per year or more getting quotes from at least four insurers before buying a car or every two years on your current policy is one of the highest-return financial moves most people never make. For fuel: at 15,000 miles per year and 30 mpg, a driver pays $1,750 to $2,250 annually at current gas prices. Electric vehicles eliminate fuel costs but carry higher insurance premiums due to repair costs. AAA estimates maintenance and repair at roughly 4 to 5 cents per mile $600 to $750 per year for the average driver. A transmission replacement or catalytic converter can cost $2,000 to $5,000.

## The Rule of Thumb

Before you buy any car, calculate the full cost of ownership, not just the payment. A good rule of thumb: your total annual car expenditure should not exceed 20% of your gross income. If you earn $60,000 a year, $12,000 is your ceiling for all car costs not just the payment. Most people fail this test. They buy a $500-a-month car payment and are then surprised when the insurance is $150 a month and the fuel and maintenance add another $200. The car you can afford is not the car the dealer approves you for. It is the car that fits the full cost of ownership within your budget and leaves room for retirement savings, an emergency fund, and the life you are actually trying to build.
